# Build on what worked

An agent asked for "a website for the new client" starts from a blank page, even when the same team shipped a site for a client in the same niche last month, with ads converting. The new one comes out with fewer sections, no conversion tag, a weaker call to action. It is not wrong, just worse than what the team already knew how to do. This skill makes the proven piece the floor: **start from what worked, meet every point it had, beat it on at least one, and show the comparison.**

## 1. Before starting: did we already make something like this that worked?

1. Search the project folders, the same client's past work, and clients in the same niche for something equal or similar that **worked** (conversions, sales, a better cost) or that **the owner approved**:
   - website or landing page;
   - approved proposal;
   - video or post that won in ads;
   - live ad campaign;
   - sales-assistant prompt that closes.
2. If it exists, **that is the base.** Copy its full structure (sections, order, buttons, tracking, sales-copy pattern, checks) and improve it. Do not start from zero.
3. Start from zero only when **the owner asks for something new** in their own words, or nothing similar exists. Then write one line: "No proven base for this; I searched X and Y."
4. Write at the top of the work: "Base: <path or link> (why: <result or approval>)".

**Brand and repetition:** from the base, copy what makes it work: structure, sections, order, conversion, tracking, speed, checks. The **look** follows this client's own brand (logo, colors, existing style). Two clients never look identical: the same structure that sells, with a different face. Never carry over another client's photos, copy, prices, contact details, data or private material; reuse only your own team's structure and process, and keep every required approval.

## 2. List what the base has

Before building, list EVERYTHING the base has. For a website, for example:
- sections and their order;
- call or chat buttons with the right number, and how many;
- the conversion tag and its label;
- headlines with the words people search for;
- prices and facts that match the ad exactly;
- fast load on a phone;
- real photos of the client;
- form or call path, privacy notice;
- anything the owner corrected on this kind of piece before.

The new piece must have **every point (a check per point)** and **at least one thing better**, stated concretely ("loads in 0.8 s vs 1.4 s", "adds real reviews", "sticky chat button on mobile").

## 3. No shipping at 50%

Forbidden in a delivery:
- "version 1", "first draft", "rough cut" (unless the owner asked for a draft);
- "I'll do the rest later", "needs polish", "I'll add X next";
- empty sections, placeholder text, buttons or links that go nowhere, stand-in photos.

When something truly cannot be done (a missing fact, photo or access, a third party): do **everything else** completely, and say in **ONE line** what is missing, why, and who must supply it. Mark that gap visibly in the piece so nobody publishes it by mistake.

## 4. Before delivering: side by side

Show this table with the delivery:

```
Base: <path or link>
| Point from the base | Base | New | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... | yes / no (why) |
Better than the base: <what, with the number or proof>
Missing: <nothing, or one line>
```

- Verify every check on the real result: the live page (curl, browser, phone width), the opened PDF, the video frames, the campaign read back.
- A "no" is not "done": fix it, or explain in one line why it does not apply.
- Also check the look against the base: it should not be a visual copy.

## 5. When finished

- Report at the real local time with the table and the proof.
- If the new piece beats the base and the owner approves it, it becomes the new base. Write that down where the other sessions will see it.

## What this prevents

- Designing from zero when a proven template from the same niche existed.
- Shipping less than the team already knew how to do.
- A "version 1" that never gets finished.
- Saying "done" without comparing against what already worked.
- Copying the base so closely that two clients look the same.

Works with a do-it-now rule (start in the same turn) and a verification-before-completion rule (check before saying done).
